20-minute home workout for beginners (no equipment)

By the Super Fit Women team · Updated 18 November 2026

Share this guide WhatsApp
In short
  • Three times a week, 20 minutes each: a 4-minute warm-up, 12 minutes of six simple moves and a 4-minute cool-down.
  • No equipment and no jumping. A chair, a wall and a little floor space are enough.
  • Start easy: 30 seconds of work and 30 seconds of rest. Make it harder only when it starts to feel easy.

Before you start

Clear a space the size of a yoga mat, keep a glass of water nearby and wear clothes you can move in. Shoes are optional on a soft floor.

If you are pregnant, have recently had a baby, or have heart problems, high blood pressure, joint pain or any other health condition, check with your doctor first. Our pregnancy and postpartum programs are made for those stages.

Warm-up (4 minutes)

  • March on the spot (1 minute). Lift your knees and swing your arms. Speed up a little in the last 20 seconds.
  • Arm circles (1 minute). Small circles forward for 30 seconds, then backward.
  • Hip circles (1 minute). Hands on your hips, draw slow circles, 30 seconds each way.
  • Slow squats to a chair (1 minute). Sit back until you just touch the chair, then stand up.

The workout (12 minutes)

Do each move for 40 seconds, then rest for 20 seconds. After all six moves, rest for a minute and do the round again. In your first two weeks, use 30 seconds of work and 30 seconds of rest instead.

  1. Chair squat. Feet hip-width apart. Sit back as if into a chair, touch it lightly and stand up. Keep your knees in line with your toes and your chest up.
  2. Wall push-up. Hands on a wall at shoulder height, step your feet back and lower your chest towards the wall. Too easy? Do push-ups on your knees on the floor.
  3. Glute bridge. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Squeeze your glutes and lift your hips until your body makes a straight line from knees to shoulders. Lower slowly.
  4. Reverse lunge. Step one foot back and bend both knees, then return. Switch legs each time. Hold a wall or chair for balance if you need to.
  5. Bird dog. On your hands and knees, reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back. Hold for two seconds, then switch sides. Keep your back flat.
  6. Step jacks. A jumping jack without the jump: step one foot out to the side while you raise both arms, then step back in. Switch sides and keep a steady pace.

Cool-down (4 minutes)

Walk slowly around the room for a minute, then hold each stretch for about 30 seconds while breathing slowly: a hamstring stretch (one heel forward, lean gently over the straight leg) on each side, a chest stretch with your hands clasped behind your back, and child's pose on the floor.

How often, and how to make it harder

Do this workout on three days that aren't next to each other, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday. On the other days, a 20 to 30 minute walk is a great partner. The World Health Organization recommends that adults get 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week and do muscle-strengthening exercise on at least two days. This plan covers the strength part.

  • Weeks 1–2: 30 seconds of work, 30 seconds of rest, one or two rounds.
  • Weeks 3–4: 40 seconds of work, 20 seconds of rest, two rounds.
  • After that: add a third round, or choose harder versions: full squats without the chair, push-ups on your knees, single-leg bridges.
Stop and get medical advice if you feel chest pain, dizziness, sharp joint pain or unusual shortness of breath. Mild muscle soreness for a day or two after a new workout is normal.
