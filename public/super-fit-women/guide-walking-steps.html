Walking for weight loss: how many steps a day do you need?

By the Super Fit Women team · Updated 18 November 2026

Share this guide WhatsApp
In short
  • 10,000 steps isn't a magic number. Large studies show the health benefits keep growing up to about 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day for adults under 60, and 6,000 to 8,000 for people over 60.
  • For weight loss, walking helps, but what you eat matters more. Walking makes a small calorie deficit easier to keep.
  • Start from your own number. Add 1,000 to 2,000 steps a day, and keep that for a week before adding more.

Where does 10,000 steps come from?

The number became popular in Japan in the 1960s, with a step counter whose name means "10,000-step meter". It was a catchy goal, not a medical one. A 2022 analysis of 15 studies with almost 50,000 adults found that people who walked more lived longer, with the benefit levelling off at about 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day for adults under 60 and 6,000 to 8,000 for older adults. So if 10,000 feels out of reach, every extra thousand still counts.

How many calories does walking burn?

For most women, every 1,000 steps burns roughly 30 to 45 kcal. You burn more if you weigh more, walk faster or walk uphill. So 3,000 extra steps a day adds up to about 100 to 130 kcal: a useful push, but not a full weight-loss plan on its own. Pair your walks with small food changes, such as fewer sugary drinks and fried snacks, and the results add up much faster.

Find your starting point

Look at your average steps over the last seven days, from your phone or the step counter in our app. Then add 1,000 to 2,000 steps a day on top of that average. Once it feels normal, usually after a week, add another 1,000. Keep going until you are around 7,000 to 8,000 steps or more on most days.

Easy ways to add steps

  • A 10-minute walk after meals. Research shows that light walking after eating lowers the rise in blood sugar, and after dinner it's a lovely habit to share with family.
  • Walk while you talk. Take phone calls on your feet, at home or on the terrace.
  • Take the stairs instead of the lift for a floor or two.
  • Walk to nearby errands: the vegetable market, the grocery shop or school pick-up.
  • Book a walking date with a friend or your partner in the morning or evening. You're more likely to go when someone is waiting.
  • Walk indoors on rainy days or hot afternoons: march on the spot or walk around the house during TV ads.

Does speed matter?

Yes. A brisk walk, where you can talk but couldn't sing, counts as moderate activity. The World Health Organization recommends 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week, which is about 30 minutes of brisk walking on five days. Slow steps still count towards your daily total.

Walk safely. Wear comfortable shoes with good support. In summer, walk early in the morning or after sunset, carry water and avoid the midday heat. If you have joint, heart or breathing problems, ask your doctor what pace is right for you.

Track your steps

The Super Fit Women app counts your steps with your phone's step sensor or Health Connect, shows distance and calories, and lets you set a daily goal that grows with you. See everything in the Health tab on the features page.

Sources

  • The acute effects of interrupting prolonged sitting with standing and light walking on cardiometabolic health (Buffey et al., Sports Medicine, 2022)
  • WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour (World Health Organization, 2020)
