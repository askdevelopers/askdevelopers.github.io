How to lose belly fat at home: what really works

By the Super Fit Women team · Updated 18 November 2026

Share this guide WhatsApp
In short
  • You can't burn fat from one spot. Crunches make your core stronger, but belly fat goes down when your overall body fat goes down.
  • What works: a small, steady calorie deficit, daily movement with strength training two or three times a week, protein and fibre at every meal, good sleep and less stress.
  • Measure your waist, not just your weight. It shows progress that the scale can miss.

The myth: "do more crunches"

Many people try to lose belly fat with hundreds of sit-ups. In a 2011 study, adults did seven ab exercises five days a week for six weeks. Their core got stronger, but their belly fat didn't change. Your body decides where it loses fat from, mostly based on your genes and hormones, and for many women the belly is one of the last places. That isn't a reason to skip core work: a strong core helps your posture and protects your back. It just won't melt fat on its own.

What really works

  1. Eat a little less than you burn. A small, steady deficit beats a crash diet. Losing about half a kilo a week is a realistic pace that most people can keep up.
  2. Move every day, and train strength two or three times a week. Walking burns calories, and strength exercises such as squats, bridges and push-ups keep your muscle while you lose fat. Try our 20-minute home workout.
  3. Add protein and fibre to every meal. Dal, chana, rajma, sprouts, paneer, curd, eggs, fish or chicken, with plenty of vegetables and salad. They keep you full for longer.
  4. Cut down on sugar and refined flour. Sweets, sugary drinks, packaged juices, biscuits, and snacks made with maida or deep-fried add calories quickly. If you take sugar in your tea, reduce it a little each week.
  5. Sleep 7 to 9 hours. Short sleep increases hunger and cravings the next day.
  6. Look after your stress. A walk, a few minutes of slow breathing, yoga or time with friends all help. Long-term stress makes healthy habits harder to keep.

Measure your waist

Once a month, measure around your waist halfway between your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone, after breathing out normally. Keep the tape level and snug, not tight. For South Asian women, a waist of 80 cm (31.5 inches) or more is linked to a higher risk of diabetes and heart disease, so it's a useful number to track even if your weight looks fine.

How long does it take?

Most people notice a difference in how their clothes fit after four to eight weeks of steady habits. Weight goes up and down from day to day with water and food, so look at the trend over weeks. Monthly waist measurements and photos tell a truer story than the daily scale.

Talk to your doctor if you put on weight quickly without a clear reason, or if you have PCOS, thyroid problems, irregular periods or symptoms of menopause. These can make belly fat harder to lose, and the right treatment helps.
