How a ride works

One car. Six stations.

The car keeps to its own route. Riders get on and off along the way.

  1. 01 · Driver

    Offer your seats

    Add your daily route, the time you leave and the seats you have free. Once, or on repeat weekdays.

  2. 02 · Rider

    Match on the way

    You see cars whose route passes within a short walk of your pickup and your drop.

  3. 03 · Rider

    Pick your seat

    See who already sits where, then ask for a seat. The driver accepts or declines.

  4. 04 · Both

    Say the code

    At pickup you tell the driver your 4-digit code, so you always get into the right car.

  5. 05 · Rider

    Pay your share

    Only the kilometres you rode, straight to the driver by UPI or cash.

  6. 06 · Both

    Rate both ways

    Drivers rate riders and riders rate drivers, so good co-travellers stand out.

Fair share

Pay for your kilometres. Not one more.

The driver picks a rate between ₹2 and ₹8 per km. Each rider pays that rate for the part of the route they actually ride. RideSaathi takes nothing.

Questions

Before you ride.

Do I need a car?

No. Without a car you join as a rider. With one you can offer seats, and the same account can do both.

How is my share worked out?

The kilometres you ride in the car times the driver's rate per km, between ₹2 and ₹8. You see the exact amount before you ask for a seat.

Does RideSaathi take a cut?

No. There is no commission. You pay the driver directly, by UPI or cash, and RideSaathi never handles the money.

Can I earn money with my car?

No. RideSaathi is for sharing the running costs of trips you make anyway. Shares are capped per km, and carrying people for profit in a private car isn't allowed.

The car doesn't pass my street. Can I still join?

Yes, if its route passes within a short walk of both your pickup and your drop. The app shows only cars that fit.

What are women-only rides?

Women drivers can open a ride to women only. Women riders can choose to see just those rides.

Does it work outside big cities?

Yes. It works anywhere in India. The 17 biggest metros also come with their main office hubs ready to pick.

Can I share a ride to another city?

Yes. Offer or find a seat between cities on a date, for example Pune to Mumbai, with the same per-km share.
