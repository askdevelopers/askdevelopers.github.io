Impact

Every shared seat is a car left at home.

The car was going anyway. When you ride in its empty seat instead of driving your own, a whole car trip disappears: its petrol, its CO₂, its parking space. Here is what one seat adds up to, zoomed out.

From one trip to the whole planet

  1. 01 · One trip

    1.7 kg CO₂

    Your 14 km seat in Rahul's car, instead of your own. That's 0.7 litres of petrol that never burns. Anita's 7 km next to you saves another 0.8 kg.

  2. 02 · Your year

    806 kg CO₂

    Both ways, five days a week, 48 weeks a year: 6,720 km you don't drive, 355 litres of petrol you don't buy, and a year of parking you don't pay for.

  3. 03 · Your city

    806 tonnes

    If 1,000 commuters in one city share like this: 1,000 fewer cars in the rush hour, 1,000 parking spaces free, and less exhaust on the roads you breathe beside.

  4. 04 · Your state

    80,600 tonnes

    If 1 lakh commuters across a state's cities share: lighter traffic on every office corridor and 3.5 crore litres of petrol not burned in a year.

  5. 05 · India

    8 lakh tonnes

    If 10 lakh commuters across India share: 35 crore litres of petrol a year stay in the ground. India imports most of its crude oil, so that's money that stays home too.

  6. 06 · The planet

    Every tonne counts

    Road transport makes about 12% of India's energy CO₂ and could double by 2050 on today's path. A seat in a car that's going anyway is the cheapest cut there is: nothing to build, nothing to buy.

Estimates, not measured savings. Each step assumes riders would otherwise drive a petrol car alone. The city, state and India numbers are "what if"s, not RideSaathi users. How we count ↓

More than CO₂

Good for you, your office and your city.

  1. Leave the car, skip the parking

    Riders don't pay for parking or circle the block looking for it. Offices need fewer spaces, and streets get their kerbs back.

  2. Your commute becomes your network

    Share the road with people from other companies and other fields, every weekday. Forty minutes together, day after day, turns into ideas, introductions and friendships.

  3. Good for people at work

    Employees spend less on the commute and don't sit in traffic alone. Drivers get their running costs shared. Companies need fewer parking spaces and cut their commute emissions, with no budget.

  4. Know who is in the car

    Every rider and driver signs in with Google, Apple or email. Confirm your office email and your profile shows a verified badge. Ratings from past rides sit on every profile, both ways.

Only riders' kilometres
The driver was making the trip anyway, so we count only the kilometres riders spend in the car instead of in their own. If you'd otherwise take the metro or a bus, your saving is smaller, and that's a good choice too.
